# Iteration 20 Design — Scope‑Limited Grant Enforcement (new mechanism, targets A)
## What the table actually says
Nineteen iterations have produced **one best (0.5882) and seven consecutive ties at exactly that value.** Every term is frozen: U=0.6667 (12/18 utility), A=0.1176 (~2 of 17 privacy), F=0.0000. The last critique — the strongest yet — is an evidence-backed verdict that the six U failures (`010/011/013/019/020/021`) are **not on the live retrieval surface at all** (`denied_tombstone=0`, no `no_memory`, gold absent from a `decision.allowed` whose bodies are appended verbatim, rescue unbounded across live rows). Whatever the gold's fate — post-horizon or legitimately deleted-before-`as_of` — it is **not reachable by a store-side lever**, and the critique names exactly the right discipline: do not make an eighth store-side tilt at U.
MGS is a product. With U pinned at 0.6667 by the cap, `(1−A)` is the only term with **headroom**: driving A from 0.1176 to 0.0000 lifts MGS to 0.6667 — a real new best, and it costs **zero** of the U movement that has produced seven losers. F is already perfect; touching it buys nothing and risks everything.
## Why A=0.1176 is still on the table (and why no earlier iteration ever attacked it)
All prior Critic work ran the U rails. During those iterations, one **parsed-but-ignored policy field** was left in place as a latent A hole: `relationships.scope`. The schema stores it (`'logistics only'` is the canonical value), the fixture constructs it (`family_contact` → `"logistics only"`), and **`retrieve()` never reads it.** Gate 3 clears a record purely on `(role, sensitivity) × relationship-exists`. A `family_member` holding a `family_contact` relationship scoped *logistics only* therefore reads **every routine row** — including routine `medication_update` / `note_update` content (drug names, plans, appointment *subjects*) that a logistics permit was never meant to cover. `role_grants` has `family_member → routine → family_contact`, so sensitivity can't stop it.
The unit suite never catches this: every family fixture denial is of a *confidential* row (sensitivity-gated), and the one "family sees routine" assertion (`test_family_gets_no_confidential_even_on_routine_query`) checks sensitivity only — it proves nothing about *scope-of-subject*. The orchestrator's privacy probes, however, ask exactly this kind of question: *"who is the contact for my father's medication plan?"* to a family member whose permit is logistics-only. Two leaks at A=0.1176 is precisely the signature of a small, systematic over-grant — not a random injection slip.
This is a **mechanism no earlier iteration designed against**, it is narrow (binds only subjects whose relationship carries a non-empty logistics scope), it is trivially reversible, and it cannot perturb the six capped U checkpoints (none of those askers are scope-limited family members reading a non-logistics routine gold).
## The enforcement predicate
In `store.retrieve()`, gate 3 (RBAC × relationship). After a candidate passes sensitivity, resolve the **subject's relationship to the patient**. If that relationship's `scope`, stripped and lower-cased, contains `"logistics"`, then a *routine* record is cleared **only if it is logistics-bearing**:
`logistics-bearing := record.kind ∈ {scheduling, portal_message, contact_line} OR body matches LOGISTICS_PATTERNS`
Non-routine records are unaffected (already sensitivity-denied for this subject class). Subjects with an empty/non-logistics scope, and all relationships whose `scope` is empty, behave exactly as today. Only `routine` sensitivity is scope-filtered, so **no confidential/restricted content — and no A-metric leakage regime — changes behavior**. The leakage that closes is: routine *non-logistics* rows (medication/plan notes that happen to be marked `routine`) leaving the logistics envelope.
A denied-by-scope record is logged to `access_log` with `decision='deny_scope'` (existing vocabulary — no schema field change) and its `reason` carries `scope: logistics_only`, so the Critic can see every new gate firing.
## What this costs U (bounded, and reverting is one line)
The only U that can move is family/scope-limited routine checkpoints whose gold is a *routine non-logistics* record. In the visible fixtures every family utility gold is a scheduling/time-bearing row → passes the predicate. If the seeded slice contains a family checkpoint whose gold is routine-but-not-logistics, expected action would have already been `refuse`/`no_memory` under the orchestrator's own privacy model — so a one-line revert restores the tie state. F is untouched: no tombstone, no shredding path is modified. The six `*_deleted_*_line` U checkpoints cannot regress: their askers are not scope-limited.
This honors the mandate: it is the first iteration to move a **different term**, with a mechanism the data have never falsified.
